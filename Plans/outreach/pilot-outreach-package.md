## 10. Objection Handlers — Top 8

**1. "We already use WellSky."**
> Good — keep it. WellSky is your system of record for clinical and billing. Christina sits on top as the intake action layer. We read from WellSky, write referral dispositions back to WellSky. Your team logs into one screen, your nurses still chart in WellSky. No rip-and-replace, no migration project.

**2. "AI is risky for HIPAA."**
> Agree, which is why we never use a public LLM. Our stack is AWS Bedrock under the AWS BAA, with Claude running in our own VPC — no data leaves the agency's tenant boundary. Vendor stack: AWS, WorkOS for auth, Deepgram for voice, Twilio for SMS — every one signed BAA before we touch a row of PHI. We can send you our compliance one-pager before the kickoff call.

**3. "We don't have IT staff."**
> Christina is a hosted SaaS — no on-prem install, no server you maintain. We do the eFax provisioning, the Twilio number purchase, the WorkOS SSO config. Your team gets a login URL on day one. Implementation lift on your side is naming the pilot intake user and signing the BAA.

**4. "Too expensive."**
> The pilot is free for 60 days, one seat. After that, $499 per intake seat per month. Math: one referral that converts to a 60-day episode of care nets the agency around $3,200 in revenue. If Christina recovers one extra conversion per month per seat, the seat pays for itself six times over. We can run the leakage math against your actual numbers before you decide.

**5. "We tried Forcura."**
> Forcura — now Mosai after the Medalogix merger — is a document summarizer. Christina is an action layer with documents as one input. Mosai tells you what the fax says; Christina drafts the intake, runs the eligibility check, queues the thank-you, and tracks BD-rep attribution. Different layer, often complementary.

**6. "You're a startup."**
> Correct. Which is why the pilot is free, the BAA is signed before any data moves, and the contract has a 30-day exit clause. We carry the risk on the first 60 days; you decide on day 61. If we don't move your time-to-SOC needle, you walk and we keep the relationship.

**7. "What about EHR integration?"**
> Phase 1 is read-only sync from WellSky / HCHB / Axxess via their REST APIs and SFTP exports — patient roster, referral disposition, episode status. Phase 2 (Q3) is bidirectional write-back to disposition fields. We do not touch clinical charting — that stays in your EHR. Integration spec is in our tech doc; happy to send.

**8. "Show me references."**
> Fair. We're early — three pilot agencies running today, all under NDA for the first 90 days. I can connect you with the Director of Intake at our flagship pilot once she's past day 60 and has hard numbers to share — she's at day 38 right now. In the meantime, the demo is live and clickable, and the architecture docs are open. Trust the product, then trust the references.

- Referral-to-admit conversion rate increased ≥ 10 percentage points
- ≥ 85% AI-drafted referral approvals with zero coordinator edits by day 30
- Zero TCPA, HIPAA, or CIPA violations recorded

## Appendix — Compliance Notes for Outreach Operators

- **Do not send cold email into California-only-recording counties for the voice-feature pitch.** CIPA two-party consent risk applies the moment we mention recorded calls. Use the OCR / time-to-SOC hooks instead, defer voice pitch until BAA + state-recording disclosure script is signed.
- **Honor every unsubscribe request immediately.** Log to the `response_status = closed_unqualified` field with `objection_raised = other` and the literal opt-out text in `notes`.
- **Never include PHI in any outbound message.** All examples in the demo are synthetic with `data-demo="true"` markers and `DEMO-####` MRNs.
- **Sender domain warm-up:** ramp from 20 → 50 → 100 sends per day over the first 14 days of any new outbound domain. Use a dedicated subdomain (e.g., `outbound.aidegens.com`) so the primary domain stays clean.
- **SPF, DKIM, DMARC required** before touch 1 of any cadence — deliverability collapses otherwise.
